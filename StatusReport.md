We generated checksums for both datasets and have added them as part of data acquisition.

These three tables was then used to create a final graph containing the following variables: year, total_loss_musd, total_deaths, total_injuries, avg_severity, gdp_real_usd, world_population, co2_tons, and co2_per_capita. Utilizing DuckDB makes it more convenient to comput metrics such as average yearly severity of any changing economic trends. Creating these tables and graph also assists in simple but efficient aggregation of statistics such as yearly climate disasters or total economic loss per year. 

Furthermore, we have made progress in an initial cleaning, transforming, and integration of the two datasets to create a compact dataframe that joined yearly disasters with GDP and carbon emission values. To clean the dataframes, we converted the dates into datetime format, removed any missing entries, retitled column names, and ensured standardized value formatting. Additionally, we created computed variables such as "impact per person usd" which was created using our script. Any empty or invalid values were removed and replaced. The dataset was aggregated by year which was used to calculate the average statistics such as total number of events, total economic loss, etc. The aggregation of datasets resulted in our master dataset which will serve as the foundation for the remainder of our project.

So far, we have completed evaluation of completeness and consistency with our intial data analysis. In terms of completeness, both datasets contain few missing values, which was addressed through cleaning. Both dataset were also highly consistent. Any inconsistencies or missing values were addressed through formatting and processing. The disater dataset covers years 202-2025 while GDP covers a wider range of years. Because of the mismatch of ranges, we intend to focus only on the years that overlap. Overall, both datasets have very few missing values and most columns have consistent formatting espeically after cleaning. To further evaluate the data for accuracy we will cross-reference with other trusted sources on natural disasters. For accuracy, we plan to cross-validate the statistics we have already obtains with outside trusted sources.
